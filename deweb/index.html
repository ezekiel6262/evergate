Ada's gate

All good

Ada is checking in. Nothing in this gate can move without Ada.

The rule, in plain words

  1. Check in every month.
  2. If 3 check-ins in a row are missed, about 3 months of silence, alert the guardians and heirs and start a wait of 1 month.
  3. A check-in at any point before release cancels the alert.
  4. Release only when 2 of 3 guardians approve and the wait is over.
  5. Then pay Theo 60% and June 40% of every asset in the gate.

While Ada is checking in, Ada can replace this plan. That publishes a new rule and moves the assets. Nobody else can change it, including Evergate. Once an alert has started, the plan stays until Ada checks in. After the guardians agree and the wait ends, Ada can no longer cancel.

How the clock becomes two answers

The rule cannot read the clock. EvergateKeeper turns the timestamps into two answers, then asks the rule. That conversion is the whole of it:

missed = 0 when now is at or before the last check-in
       = the whole intervals since then, and never more than 15
wait   = true only after an alert has started,
         and now is at or after that start plus the wait

Intervals and the wait are ordinary lengths of time, counted in seconds. On this gate an interval is 30 days and the wait is 30 days. Three missed check-ins is about three months of silence.

The keeper then packs ten bits, least-significant bit first: missed (4 bits), the owner's check-in, the three guardian votes, the wait answer, and whether the payout finished. How many misses are allowed, and how many guardians must agree, are fixed inside this rule. They are not in those ten bits.

A worked packing: 3 missed, no check-in, guardians 1 and 3 in favour, the wait elapsed, payout not done. The two bytes are a301.
